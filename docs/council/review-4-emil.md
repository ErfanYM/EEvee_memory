[blocker] The `::after` hit area is offset outward, so every print's tap target is bigger than the print and steals taps from the visible photo or poster of neighbouring prints — css/main.css:116 `.polaroid__open::after { inset: calc(-1 * var(--print-pad)) calc(-1 * var(--print-pad)) calc(-1 * var(--print-pad-bottom)); }`. The comment says it is positioned against `.polaroid__tilt`, but `.polaroid__open` is not positioned, so the pseudo is placed against `.polaroid__tilt`'s padding box, which already includes the padding. A negative inset therefore pushes it a further 10px (sides/top) and 36px (bottom) at ≤1023, and 14px/50px at ≥1024 (hero 16/56px) outside the visible white frame. Evidence: Chromium 390 touch, a real `touchscreen.tap` at (102, 516) on the visible photo of `37c83b77` opened `img_8394` instead (`.superpowers/shots/review-4/emil/steal-tap.png`). A scan of every print's own visible frame at 390 finds hits stolen by another print's stretched target on 13 prints, and for 11 of them the stolen points are inside the victim's photo window: `0f88d984` (a video) 549, `img_4837` 473, `img_0826` 427, `img_4144` 351, `20221006` 325, `c1a73628` (a video) 301, `img_3785` 298, `img_7522` 265, `img_6802` 243, `23983ab7` (a video) 63, `37c83b77` 44 (3-sample grid points, roughly 3–8% of each print). At 1440, in the S2 and S6 overlaps, the upper print (`img_7207`, `img_3786`) takes 1570 and 1317 two-pixel samples from the lower print's visible area, and the tap region extends about 15px beyond the left/bottom of the upper print into empty field. Videos are the worst case: a tap on a video's poster can open a photo instead of playing it. Fix: `inset: 0` (the pseudo is absolutely positioned against `.polaroid__tilt`, whose border box is exactly the white frame):
